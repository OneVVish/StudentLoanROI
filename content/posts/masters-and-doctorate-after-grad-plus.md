Until July 1, 2026, a graduate student could borrow the whole cost of a master's or a doctorate from the government: $20,500 a year as a Direct Unsubsidized loan and anything above it as Grad PLUS. Grad PLUS is gone. A graduate student starting now can borrow **$20,500 a year and $100,000 in total** for graduate study, at 8.07 percent for loans made this school year. Undergraduate borrowing does not count against that $100,000. Anything past it comes from a private lender, an employer, a family or the program itself.

A student already enrolled on June 30, 2026, who already had a Direct loan for that program, keeps the old rules for the lesser of three more academic years or what is left of the program. Everyone starting in 2027 is under the new ones.

Medicine, dentistry, law and the other professional degrees have a separate, higher limit and their [own guide](/guides/professional-school-debt). This one is about everything else.

Money figures below are rounded to the nearest hundred dollars, except the federal limits and $41,000, which is two years at the annual limit.

## What a graduate year costs

The federal source for graduate prices is IPEDS, and it publishes tuition and fees only. At the median school a year of graduate tuition and fees is about $14,100. At a public university it is about $10,600 for a resident and $20,300 for everyone else; at a private nonprofit, about $16,200. 523 of the 1,987 schools that report a figure charge more than $20,500 in tuition and fees alone, at their resident price.

That figure is also an average across every graduate program at the school. An engineering master's and an education master's at the same university collapse into one number, so a particular program can sit well above or below it.

IPEDS publishes no living costs for graduate students at all. The closest published figure is what each school budgets an undergraduate for housing, food and books, about $14,400 a year at the median. Add it to graduate tuition and **1,330 of the 1,559 schools with both figures, 85 percent, cost more than $20,500 a year**. At the median the year comes to a bit under $28,000, about $7,400 more than the federal loan will cover, or roughly $15,000 over a two-year master's.

## What graduates borrowed before the limit

The College Scorecard publishes, program by program, the median federal debt of graduates who borrowed for that credential. Those figures come from before July 2026, so they include Grad PLUS, and they exist only for programs large enough to publish one. They show what the typical borrower needed, which is the thing to set against $41,000.

| Master's field | Programs reporting | Typical program's median debt | Share above $41,000 | Above $100,000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health professions | 864 | $48,100 | 60 percent | 108 |
| Psychology | 338 | $45,200 | 57 percent | 10 |
| Visual and performing arts | 132 | $41,000 | 44 percent | 10 |
| Public administration and social service | 406 | $39,300 | 31 percent | 0 |
| Computer and information sciences | 163 | $38,900 | 35 percent | 0 |
| MBA alone | 779 | $35,400 | 27 percent | 5 |
| Business, all master's | 867 | $33,000 | 22 percent | 1 |
| Education | 728 | $29,600 | 12 percent | 0 |

The two business rows overlap: the second counts every business master's, MBAs included, so the MBA row is a closer look at part of it rather than an addition. Across every master's program reporting, 4,688 of them at 1,366 schools, the typical median is about $38,000 and 35 percent of programs sit above $41,000. Health and psychology are the fields the limit reaches first: in both, the typical graduate who borrowed needed more than two years of the new federal loan.

Doctorates run higher and longer. Across 591 doctoral programs at 434 schools the typical median is about $79,200, and 217 of them, 37 percent, sit above $100,000, the whole federal total for graduate study. Psychology doctorates are the extreme: a typical median of about $129,600, with 49 of 77 programs past $100,000. Doctorates in the health professions come to about $86,700, with 118 of 288 past it.

Whether a particular doctorate counts as a professional degree, with the $200,000 limit rather than $100,000, depends on the program and on a federal definition that has been in dispute this year. The school's aid office can say which limit applies.

## When a master's career comes out ahead

The calculator carries 41 careers that the Bureau of Labor Statistics says typically need a master's. Each was run at national wages, 6.5 percent, on the Repayment Assistance Plan, with the years of school counted, at two levels of total borrowing for both degrees: $27,000, the most a dependent undergraduate can borrow federally with nothing borrowed for the master's, and $68,000, which adds two full years at the graduate limit.

| Total borrowed | Behind a debt-free high school graduate after ten years | Median age at which the career gets ahead for good |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $27,000 | 19 of 41 | 32 |
| $68,000 | 20 of 41 | 32 |

The extra $41,000 moved one career across the line. The split between careers is far wider than anything the loan does. Nurse anesthetists, physician assistants, nurse practitioners and nurse midwives are ahead by their late twenties. Statisticians, occupational therapists and speech-language pathologists get there around 30. Mental health counselors, school and career counselors, social workers in mental health and librarians do not pull ahead until their late thirties or forties, and rehabilitation counselors are still behind 40 years out.

What moves those ages is the time. With the school years left out, so that both people are compared from the day the degree ends, only 2 of the 41 careers are behind at ten years. Six years of school earning little or nothing, set against six years of a high school graduate's wages, is most of the cost of a master's. The loan is the smaller part.

## What this does not count

- **A bachelor's is the real alternative.** The calculator measures every path against a debt-free high school graduate. For most people weighing a master's, the comparison that matters is against the job they would hold with the bachelor's they already have, and that is a different and usually harder test.
- **Money that is not a loan.** Employer tuition benefits, assistantships and funded doctoral places pay for school instead of lending for it, and research doctorates in particular are often funded.
- **Living costs are an estimate.** The undergraduate allowance is the closest published figure, not a graduate budget.
- **A doctorate's length.** Real programs run four to eight years, and the calculator's five-year default is a placeholder that it lets you change.
