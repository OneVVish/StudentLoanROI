Some families filed the 2026-27 FAFSA expecting the same answer as the year before and got a Student Aid Index a fraction of the size. Nothing was wrong with the form. The July 2025 budget law, Public Law 119-21, rewrote the list of assets the federal formula is allowed to see, and for a family that owns a small business or lives on its farm, the list got a lot shorter.

SAI figures in this guide come from [this site's SAI tool](/?tool=sai&from=guide), which runs the published 2027-28 federal worksheet, and are rounded to the nearest hundred dollars.

## What the law took out

Section 80001 of the law amends section 480(f)(2) of the Higher Education Act, the paragraph that says what "assets" leaves out. Before, it excluded one thing, the family home. Starting with the 2026-27 award year it excludes three more:

- a family farm on which the family resides;
- a small business with not more than 100 full-time or full-time equivalent employees, or any part of one, that is owned and controlled by the family;
- a commercial fishing business, including vessels and permits, owned and controlled by the family.

The 2024-25 and 2025-26 forms counted all three. The law that redesigned the FAFSA had dropped an older version of the exclusion, so for two award years a family's business sat on the worksheet beside its savings. The 2027-28 form is the second year with them gone.

Two conditions do the work in that list. The business has to be owned and controlled by the family, which a passive stake in somebody else's company is not. And the headcount ceiling is 100, counting full-time equivalents, so a firm of 140 people stays on the form whoever owns it.

## What it was worth

A business that counts does not count at face value. The worksheet first runs its net worth through a sliding scale, Table A3, which takes 40% of the first $180,000 and more of each slice after that: a $500,000 business enters as roughly $232,000. That figure then joins the family's other assets, and for 2027-28 the formula assesses parent assets at 12% from the first dollar, with no allowance protecting any of them.

For a two-parent family of four with one child in college and no savings, here is the same 2027-28 worksheet with the business counted and with it left out:

| Parents' income | Business left out | $250,000 business counted | $500,000 counted | $1,000,000 counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $60,000 | $600 | $3,500 | $7,100 | $24,200 |
| $90,000 | $6,100 | $10,000 | $16,700 | $35,500 |
| $150,000 | $25,200 | $31,300 | $38,300 | $57,200 |

At $90,000 of income a $500,000 business is the difference between an index of about $6,100 and one of about $16,700, roughly $10,600 a year of expected contribution per child in college. The gap grows faster than the business does. A $1,000,000 business adds nearly three times what a $500,000 one adds, because Table A3's slices get steeper toward the top.

That is the scale of what families with a qualifying business stopped being charged. It is also the scale of the error if one types that business into a calculator that still counts it.

## What still counts

The exclusion covers the value of the business, not the money it makes. Profit that reaches the parents' tax return is income, and income is most of the formula: it arrives as total parent income near the top of the worksheet, whether it came from a paycheck or a storefront. A family whose business pays it well will still see that on its SAI.

Everything outside the three categories stays on the form as well. Savings, investments and real estate other than the home and a farm the family lives on are parent assets at 12%. So is a business over the headcount ceiling, which enters through Table A3 exactly as the table above shows.

## Two Pell rules from the same law

The law changed Pell Grant eligibility in two ways that interact with a lower index, both effective July 1, 2026.

A student whose SAI equals or exceeds twice the maximum Pell Grant for the year is not eligible for Pell at all. The maximum is set by appropriations each year, and the 2027-28 figure has not been published, so this guide does not name the line. For a family near it, though, the business exclusion can be what moves the index from one side to the other.

A student whose grants from states, colleges or private sources equal or exceed the full cost of attendance is not eligible for Pell for that period either. A full ride from the school now replaces Pell rather than stacking on top of it.

## The schools that still ask

About 250 colleges, most of them private, also collect the CSS Profile and award their own money on a formula of their own. Nothing in the federal law reaches it. Many of those schools ask about a family business and the farm the family lives on, and some count them, so at a Profile school the federal index is a floor rather than an estimate. No published formula exists for what an individual school will do with the answer, which is why this site's tool names the direction and never an amount.

## Run your own

Enter a business or farm only if it falls outside the three categories above; the box says which to leave out. If yours counts, enter its net worth and the tool applies Table A3 for you.
